- 20260801T230609 [implemented] command:change plan:20260801T230609-owner-bound-per-intent-escrow — Escrow was recorded as a bare intentId to amount mapping, which does not establish who owns an escrow. The EIP-712 scheme does not bind an intentId to an LP: _verifyMintIntent recovers a signer and compares it to a caller-supplied lp argument, so any party can produce a signature that verifies over any intentId by signing with their own key and naming their own address. Proven against the built code: submitting a second depositForIntent for an already-funded intentId under a DIFFERENT lp reverts with DepositAlreadyEscrowed rather than InvalidSignature, so the foreign signature passed verification. Consequence on the planned consumption paths, which were specified to check only the amount: mintPositionFor would mint LP B a position funded by LP A deposit, and reclaimDeposit, which is permissionless by design and whose intentIds are published in the DepositEscrowed log, would let an attacker self-sign over a victim pending intentId, wait out RECLAIM_TIMELOCK, take the refund, and lock the victim out via usedIntents. That is the same vault drain the escrow model was introduced to close, re-entering through a different door. Fix: the escrow entry becomes a struct holding the depositor address plus the amount, packed into one slot as address plus uint96 with an inline SafeCast, and every consuming path checks the recorded depositor before moving funds. Adds FR-45I9, FR-45IA, FR-45ID, FR-45IF, SC-45IB, SC-45IE, SC-45IG, SC-45IH and ADR-45IC; FR-3Z9M, FR-3Z9N, FR-3Z9O, FR-3Z9W, FR-JAIQ, FR-3ZVM and FR-3ZVO restated against the struct. Found in review by the repo owner while reading the T-001 implementation.
